Ch 1 · Research

Two questions drive my work

Many older adults with epilepsy notice problems with memory and thinking, and they face a higher risk of later dementia. We don't yet know why. Seizures, medications, and other illnesses may all play a part, and Alzheimer's disease may be quietly present in some patients years before symptoms are obvious.

Question 1

Why does cognitive decline happen?

What changes in the brain explain memory and thinking problems in older adults with epilepsy.

Question 2

Do conditions like Alzheimer's disease contribute?

Whether early Alzheimer's pathology drives both the seizures and the cognitive symptoms in some patients.

Ch 2 · Current projects

Active projects

  • Blood biomarkers

    Blood biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease in older adults with epilepsy

    We use validated and investigational blood tests to see whether Alzheimer's-related changes are present in patients with epilepsy.

  • Cognition over time

    How cognition changes over time

    We follow memory and thinking in older adults with epilepsy over several years to learn who develops cognitive problems and how quickly.

  • Epilepsy types

    Are specific types of epilepsy caused by early Alzheimer's disease?

    Some types of epilepsy may be an early sign of Alzheimer's disease rather than a separate condition, and we are working to identify them.

  • Prediction

    Can we predict who will develop dementia?

    We combine clinical, biomarker, and EEG information to identify the patients at highest risk of developing cognitive problems.

  • Brain signals

    Brain signals behind cognitive decline

    We use EEG to search for electrical signatures that explain why someone with epilepsy is losing cognitive function.

Long term

  • Treatment

    Are there medications that treat cognitive symptoms in epilepsy?

    Our goal is to turn what we learn about causes into clinical trials of treatments that protect memory and thinking.
